# Review 34 — pacing versus loss recovery

**Verdict:** pacing has a large, reproducible effect in this particular software harness. F37 shows no clear throughput benefit on one real path, despite strong evidence that pacing engaged. Neither establishes which feature matters most for ordinary exit-node use. “The losses aren't caused by our bursts” and “RTOs cap the single flow at 21 Mbit/s” are unsupported causal claims. Fix demonstrated timer bugs; treat the throughput ranking as unsettled.

Read-only review of source, scripts and raw records; no new network tests or builds. I recalculated the Brief 30/32 aggregates and F37 counter differences. Benchmark source below means `scratch/pacing-ab`; the `tcp-0.14` benchmark file is byte-identical.

## 1. Is the emulated benchmark fair?

**Good internal comparison, limited external validity.** Brief 32 really compares stock 0.14.0 with a pacing-only delta: the source diff adds the pacer, its RTT gate and dispatch/timer integration, without NewReno/SACK/B1/B2 or a lower RTO. Both variants explicitly select the same controller and disable Nagle. All 432 raw records exit successfully and consistently identify one binary hash per variant. Adjacent stock/pacer pairs and three repetitions are useful safeguards ([socket policy:35](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/ts_netstack_smoltcp_core/src/socket_impl/tcp/mod.rs:35), [matrix runner:17](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/docs/pacing-ab-32-matrix.py:17), [raw records](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/docs/pacing-ab-32-results.jsonl)). This is credible evidence of an implementation effect, not evidence of its proposed mechanism in every cell.

The important qualifications:

- **The receiver is part of the experiment.** Both endpoints use the same smoltcp build, buffers and policy ([bench:191](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/ts_forwarder/benches/forward_throughput.rs:191)). The receiver retains a 10 ms delayed-ACK default; another segment can force expiry, out-of-order data elicits immediate ACKs, and a doubling of the advertised window triggers a window update. Window-changing ACKs are excluded from stock duplicate-ACK counting. These details can affect ACK clustering, fast retransmit and window reopening. They are held constant, but pacing changes their inputs. This does not test a macOS/Linux/phone receiver ([TCP:304](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/vendor/smoltcp/src/socket/tcp.rs:304), [TCP:2274](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/vendor/smoltcp/src/socket/tcp.rs:2274), [TCP:2403](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/vendor/smoltcp/src/socket/tcp.rs:2403), [TCP:2115](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/vendor/smoltcp/src/socket/tcp.rs:2115)). Enabling the pacer at the receiver does **not** explicitly pace pure ACKs; their timing still follows data arrival, application draining and polling.
- **The bottleneck receives software-speed bursts.** The pipes are unbounded and the poller repeatedly emits until blocked. The relay computes a virtual byte backlog from `link_free`, tail-drops admissions, then delivers through `sleep_until`. There is no input-link serialization, WireGuard, NIC/driver, radio scheduler or deliberate Wi-Fi ACK aggregation. Conversely, overdue software timers can deliver packets together, so it is not an ideal continuously serialized wire either. Shared CPU scheduling can change both queue admission and ACK timing ([pipe:125](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/ts_netstack_smoltcp/src/pipe.rs:125), [poller:892](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/ts_netstack_smoltcp_core/src/lib.rs:892), [relay:116](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/ts_forwarder/benches/forward_throughput.rs:116)). A real fast-to-slow link can create the same problem; its severity here has not been calibrated against one.
- **The matrix concentrates on conditions likely to reward smoothing.** At 25 ms, nominal BDP is about 458/763 KiB at 150/250 Mbit/s. Every queue, 64–256 KiB, is below BDP; its drain time is only 2.1–14.0 ms. A 1 MiB buffer exceeds BDP, while 256 KiB constrains a single flow. All eight flows start together. No random loss, independent cross traffic, variable RTT, asymmetry or AQM is exercised. These are legitimate stress cases, not a sample of typical client paths ([runner:17](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/docs/pacing-ab-32-matrix.py:17), [bench:379](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/ts_forwarder/benches/forward_throughput.rs:379)). The pacer remains bursty: cwnd/8 before loss, at most 32 MSS afterwards; retransmissions bypass it ([pacer:21](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/vendor/smoltcp/src/socket/tcp/pacing.rs:21), [TCP:2462](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/vendor/smoltcp/src/socket/tcp.rs:2462)).
- **Weak recovery amplifies the cost of losing packets.** Avoiding a loss that otherwise causes a one-second timeout can produce a huge pacing gain. That is a real benefit against stock, but not a clean estimate of pacing's benefit with competent recovery. B2 could also be avoided by changing window dynamics; its incidence was not counted. B1 requires failed emission, which this unbounded connected device does not normally exercise; blaming it for the benchmark collapse is unjustified. Importantly, Brief 30 still shows pacing helping *after* recovery fixes: medians 61.45 → 130.25 Mbit/s, maximum pause 0.23 → 0.04 s. So stock defects are not the whole explanation—but that comparison has one run per cell and no feature-by-feature recovery ablation ([Brief 30:18](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/TCP-WORKAROUND-30.md:18), [review 31:110](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/review-31-tcp-0.14.md:110)).
- **The no-loss gains remain unexplained.** Raw Reno RTT40/F1 runs are stock **361.0, 534.5, 458.1**, paced **679.1, 679.6, 681.8 Mbit/s**, all with zero queue drops. Across controllers/flow counts, medians improve 12.5–48.4%. This cannot be explained solely by avoiding tail loss. Plausible causes include smoother window utilization, delayed-ACK feedback and scheduler batching; no traces/cwnd/window/RTT measurements distinguish them. At 4 MiB, 458 versus 680 Mbit/s corresponds to an apparent window cycle of roughly 73 versus 49 ms, **not a measured RTT**. RTT0 is a weak pacing-overhead control because the RTT gate bypasses pacing there ([raw:409](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/docs/pacing-ab-32-results.jsonl:409), [TCP:2453](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/vendor/smoltcp/src/socket/tcp.rs:2453)).

**“51 → 112” is meaningful only with its denominator.** I reproduce Reno **51.15 → 112.05 Mbit/s**, the separate unweighted medians of 24 download-cell medians; 22/24 improve (Cubic 20/24). It is neither the median paired percentage gain nor expected user throughput. It mixes one/eight flows, two windows, two rates and three queues. Three runs can be unstable: B150/Q256/W1024/F1 stock is **109.8, 13.5, 13.7**; paced **120.7, 124.5, 123.8** ([raw:97](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/pacing-ab/docs/pacing-ab-32-results.jsonl:97)). Fixed A-before-B ordering and one machine leave scheduling/drift effects. Fifteen seconds includes startup and gives each one-second timeout substantial weight; this measures short-transfer performance, not steady state. Pause measures the worst individual flow's application I/O gap, not a node-wide outage; drop counts mix directions and probes and are not normalized for traffic volume ([definitions:39](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/PACING-AB-32.md:39)). Worst download pause remains 2.03 s with pacing, and some cells regress ([Brief 32:76](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/PACING-AB-32.md:76)). Report cell results and ranges alongside the aggregate.

The original 0.13 recovery comparison bundled controller, flight accounting, clock, recovery, RTO and pacing changes. Do not carry its go-back-N diagnosis or enormous gains wholesale into 0.14: stock fast retransmit there preserves the sent frontier ([Brief 24:7](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/TCP-RECOVERY-24.md:7), [upstream review:59](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/SMOLTCP-UPSTREAM-29.md:59)).

## 2. What F37 actually shows

Re-running the supplied analyzer gives:

| 1 on | 22, 21 | 48 | 13 | 263,281 |

Source: [laptop:2](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/bench/results/pacing-real/laptop-pacing-real-1.txt:2), [analyzer:18](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/runner/pacing-real-analyze.py:18). Singles median **21 off / 22 on**; parallel **49 / 48**; median node retransmission fraction **3.12% / 2.99%**. These are descriptive results, not an equivalence test.

**Engagement is strongly supported, attribution is incomplete.** The script waits eight seconds after the scheduled toggle and makes a throwaway port-443 connection to consume the old listener. Measured intervals start 8.5–9.3 seconds into their slots and finish before 40 seconds; all intervening snapshots show the expected creation policy. This substantially addresses the listener caveat, assuming synchronized clocks and successful requests. It does not identify the policy of every measured socket. Deferrals count repeated dispatch attempts, not packets delayed or achieved wire spacing ([laptop script:31](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/deploy/laptop/run-pacing-sched.sh:31), [counter definitions:35](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/PACING-LIVE-33.md:35)).

**There is a small alignment error.** The logger adds `log_ts` when it reads an older snapshot; the analyzer brackets that field rather than `ts_ms`. In block 1, its end snapshot is **0.258 s before** the laptop end. Bracketing the actual snapshot times changes block-1 RTOs **13 → 16**, and total RTOs **52 on / 48 off → 55 / 50**. On-block deferrals become **272,935 / 333,154 / 299,542**; the substantive result survives. Endpoint policy agreement alone is not a freshness check ([logger:15](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/runner/pacing-schedule.sh:15), [raw:179](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/bench/results/pacing-real/node-stats-20261016T1330.jsonl:179), [raw:210](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/bench/results/pacing-real/node-stats-20261016T1330.jsonl:210)).

**The RTO totals mostly belong to the parallel phase.** Using nearest snapshots at thirds of each ~30.23 s block gives approximately **1–3 events per single**, versus **11–19 in the eight-flow phase**. Example block 1: **1 / 1 / 11**; block 5: **1 / 1 / 19**. On-block single phases also show tens of thousands of deferrals, supporting engagement beyond just par8. Phase boundaries are inferred, not separately logged ([raw:180](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/bench/results/pacing-real/node-stats-20261016T1330.jsonl:180), [raw:190](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/bench/results/pacing-real/node-stats-20261016T1330.jsonl:190), [raw:199](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/bench/results/pacing-real/node-stats-20261016T1330.jsonl:199), [raw:631](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/bench/results/pacing-real/node-stats-20261016T1330.jsonl:631)).

All counters are node-wide. Background activity exists even between blocks; the test-associated increase strongly suggests bulk traffic dominates, but cannot assign each retransmission/RTO to a laptop flow. `retx_segs/data_segs` includes replay, probes and retransmissions in the denominator; it is **not wire loss probability**. RTO counts include SYN/FIN retries and failed emissions, and do not measure stall duration ([definitions:45](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/PACING-LIVE-33.md:45)).

**There is no established 21 Mbit/s hard ceiling.** One unpaced single reached **50**. Eight flows reaching 46–51 establishes aggregate capacity above most singles, but does not locate the bottleneck at the access link rather than the CDN, host leg or elsewhere. Recovery and cwnd collapse are plausible explanations for slower singles, not demonstrated ones. A fully usable 1 MiB window would require an effective ACK round of about **400 ms** to limit throughput to 21 Mbit/s; at 25–50 ms its window bound is about 336–168 Mbit/s. F37 records neither SRTT nor actual advertised window, so neither allocated-window limitation nor loaded RTT can be conclusively excluded. The older F31 RTT is not a measurement of this run. F37 reports a direct route, but the saved laptop output has no status line, and the script checks status only before the blocks: route stability cannot be independently audited from these artifacts ([F37:361](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/DECISIONS.md:361), [script:28](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/deploy/laptop/run-pacing-sched.sh:28)).

**No burst-causality conclusion follows.** Pacing may leave retransmission bursts, the radio may rebatch paced data, or its rate/burst policy may be ineffective at this bottleneck. Similar aggregate replay fractions cannot distinguish those possibilities from unrelated wireless loss. There is no queue/drop-location or packet-spacing evidence.

**Power is poor:** three blocks per variant, two correlated singles per block, fixed alternating order, one network/session and ten-second transfers including connection startup. Even three independent paired signs all favoring one policy give only two-sided exact sign-test **p = 0.25**; this schedule was not randomized anyway. The results argue against an obvious, consistent large gain *here*. They do not establish zero effect, a small-effect bound or population-wide behavior. The script also records no HTTP status/error breakdown or contemporaneous direct-path baseline ([script:18](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/deploy/laptop/run-pacing-sched.sh:18)).

## 3. Claims check

| Claim | Grade | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| “Pacing is the biggest single gain.” | **Supported within Brief 30's selected throughput matrix; unsupported generally.** | Its incremental lab gain exceeds the bundled recovery gain. Brief 32 independently shows large stock+pacer gains. Neither weights ordinary traffic, and recovery reduces maximum pauses much more. |
| “Recovery matters more for ordinary exit-node use.” | **Unsupported as a comparative empirical claim.** | Sensible engineering hypothesis; F37 never tests recovery. One null pacing session cannot establish the benefit of an untested alternative. |
| “Pacing is situational.” | **Supported, narrowly.** | Strong lab dependence and no clear F37 gain. The relevant real-world conditions remain unidentified. |
| “Deep buffers absorb bursts,” explaining F37. | **Weakly supported mechanism; unsupported diagnosis.** | Extra queue capacity can absorb a finite burst, but no F37 buffer was measured. Larger lab queues sometimes help and sometimes still collapse; pacing does not prevent every overflow. |
| Earlier “9 → 17–20 MB/s from the patched build.” | **Observed improvement supported; causal attribution weak.** | F31/F32 record ~9 MB/s at 256 KiB; F35 records mostly 16.9–20 at 1 MiB, plus 7.6. Window, recovery, pacing, controller/integration and test time changed. Larger-window failures before the patch support a recovery concern, not a feature attribution. Even F35's “Wi-Fi loss-recovery episode” is inferred from CPU falling, without a transport trace. |

## 4. What to believe and do

No defensible **measured real-world gain ranking** exists. My provisional engineering priority for phone/laptop TCP downloads is:

| Priority | Change | Confidence and limits |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **B2 timer fix** | **High** correctness priority: deterministic outstanding-data/no-timer transition. Real-world frequency unmeasured. Do not confuse fixing a rare wedge with maximizing average goodput. |
| 2 | **NewReno** | **Medium**: demonstrated missing partial-ACK repair; a relatively focused fallback for multiple losses without sender SACK. Size of real-path gain unknown. |
| 3 | **Sender SACK** | **Medium** mechanism, **low** incremental-gain evidence: can repair several holes without replaying acknowledged ranges when negotiated. No isolated comparison against NewReno here. |
| 4 | **200 ms computed minimum RTO** | **Medium** conditional benefit if genuine timeout waiting dominates; **low** confidence in this position relative to pacing. Preserve initial timeout/backoff and measure spurious retransmissions. |
| 5 | **Pacing** | **High** evidence of lab usefulness, **low** evidence for ordinary-use priority. Move it up for a demonstrated burst-sensitive bottleneck. It remains complementary to recovery. |

The order below B2 is a testable judgment, not a conclusion from F37. NewReno/SACK behavior has targeted tests ([partial ACK:3795](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/tcp-0.14/vendor/smoltcp/src/socket/tcp.rs:3795), [SACK:3858](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/tcp-0.14/vendor/smoltcp/src/socket/tcp.rs:3858)); B2 has a concrete retransmission regression ([B2:3644](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/scratch/tcp-0.14/vendor/smoltcp/src/socket/tcp.rs:3644)). Fuzz/review results support implementation robustness, not performance ranking. Review 26 found a serious SACK defect before correction; review 31 explicitly did not test live interop or spurious RTO rates ([review 26:9](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/review-26-tcp-recovery.md:9), [review 31:175](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/review-31-tcp-0.14.md:175)).

Smallest useful next experiments, in order; these are proposals, not actions performed:

1. **Same laptop path, matched recovery × pacing 2×2.** Stock/pacing off, stock/on, patched recovery/off, patched/on; same Reno, 1 MiB, Nagle, server and runtime. Randomize repeated adjacent sets. Start with six sets; continue until the paired interval resolves a predeclared useful effect, e.g. 10%, rather than declaring victory from six. Use longer single flows with startup and steady portions reported separately, a contemporaneous native/Go or direct baseline, verified direct routing, and no par8 mixed into single-flow accounting. Record per-flow policy/deferrals, bytes, cwnd, flight, SRTT/RTO, peer window, recovery events and stall durations with actual snapshot timestamps. Recovery raising throughput and shortening waits at unchanged pacing supports its path-specific value; unchanged results with a tight bound refute a large gain there. Pacing helping only stock would show interaction with weak recovery; helping patched too would show independent value. This is the minimum comparison that can rank the two *bundles* on that path.
2. **Real controlled step-down with a kernel receiver.** Wired fast sender-side link into a known 150/250 Mbit/s bottleneck; 64/256 KiB and a larger queue control; add ACK-path delay so minimum RTT exceeds 5 ms, otherwise this pacer bypasses itself. Compare pacing off/on first with recovery fixed. Capture sender and receiver sequence/timing plus bottleneck drops/occupancy. Fewer burst-correlated drops and better goodput support the pacing mechanism; verified smoothing without reduced drops/gain bounds that claim. Repeat with deliberate ACK batching and separately injected loss unrelated to bursts. Improvement under the former but recovery-dominated results under the latter distinguishes mechanisms that F37 cannot.
3. **One no-loss diagnostic before claiming a universal explanation.** Reproduce the RTT40/F1 control with ACK/window/cwnd/RTT traces; change only receiver ACK policy, then replace the smoltcp receiver with kernel TCP. Report startup and steady state separately. Gain disappearing with receiver replacement implicates harness feedback; persistence with kernel TCP supports broader usefulness. Neither outcome alone explains the original 12–48% without the trace.
4. **Only then separate recovery features.** With pacing fixed and B1/B2 corrected, compare NewReno, NewReno+SACK, and 1 s/200 ms floors. Use one controlled two-hole loss for mechanism, then the real path for incidence and value. Count unnecessary retransmissions as well as saved stall time. This can rank SACK and the RTO policy; existing bundled runs cannot.

## 5. What the pacing issue can honestly say today

It can report: **“In an in-memory two-smoltcp-endpoint forwarder benchmark using stock 0.14.0, adding an opt-in pacer raised the unweighted median of 24 download-cell medians from 51.2 to 112.1 Mbit/s (Reno), with three runs per variant/cell. Gains and regressions depended on configuration. Stock one-second recovery stalls remained. One small real-path A/B showed no clear throughput gain despite substantial pacing deferrals. We propose discussing an optional pacing API and testing it against kernel peers and controlled physical bottlenecks.”** Attach the matrix, raw records, algorithm, bypass/retransmission policy and limitations.

Do not claim expected doubling, “biggest gain” for users, diagnosed Wi-Fi burst loss, eliminated queue overflow, established fairness, or proven latency improvement. The no-loss gain is an open mechanism question. In particular, the current guide's “most of all” and “queue never overflows” overstate the evidence ([pacing guide:3](/Volumes/git/ts-exit/docs/smoltcp-guides/F3-pacing.md:3)). A design issue backed by a reproducible stress case is justified today; a general performance promise is not.
